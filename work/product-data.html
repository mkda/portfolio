Connecting physical products to data

2024 to 2026 · On

Hardware, partners and legacy systems all have to agree on what a single item is and where it is. The platform that makes them agree is a product too: its APIs and its events are services other teams rely on.

Problem
Stores, warehouses, vendors and the ERP (the company's finance and inventory system) each held their own view of an item.
Role
At On, the Zurich-based global sportswear brand, led the RFID technology workstream from factory shipment to 100+ stores, with 5 developers, 3 vendors and a CHF 250K+ yearly budget. Also led the Digital Twin platform.
Result
>99.9% item traceability, up from manual barcode reads. 40M+ yearly scans turned into events, with 70% fewer manual interventions across 100+ locations. An in-house serial-number service with projected savings of $200K+ a year.

How it works

Each item gets a unique serial, encoded in its RFID (radio-frequency identification) tag. Every enabled read point, from factory to store, can trace it one by one or in bulk, and the Digital Twin, a live digital record of every item, keeps the ERP in sync. A QR code paired with the same serial carries the item past the sale, into digital workflows such as its status changing from in stock to sold.

Before, items were tracked by manual barcode reads. Scan events now take over the manual steps in stock counts, in stock moves to virtual locations that track financial value (damaged, sellable and non-sellable goods, visual merchandising), in inbound and outbound shipments, and in the sync with the ERP.

Underneath sits an event-driven architecture built on the Electronic Product Code standard, with warehouse scan-point APIs (the interfaces other software calls), supply-chain location standards, an in-house serialisation service, and vendor capabilities brought in-house.

Decisions that mattered

Standards are product decisions

Modelling the Digital Twin on Electronic Product Code events and adopting Global Location Numbers (GLN, the standard IDs for sites and docks) looked like technical details. In practice they set how fast new partners and use cases could join.

Observe before you model

Interviews described how stores managed inventory. Watching them showed how they really did it, including their use of virtual locations. The retail data model was rebuilt before rollout, while the change was still cheap.

Exceptions are the product

Above 99.9% traceability, the remaining fraction still made most of the work across 100+ locations. A Pareto analysis of support tickets picked the few features that mattered, and that cut manual interventions by 70%.

Where it fits in your company

Recalls narrowed to the exact batch, checks against counterfeits, stock accurate enough for buy online and pick up in store, and the Digital Product Passport the EU is introducing for textiles and other goods.
