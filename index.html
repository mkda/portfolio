01 · What I do

Three problems I'm good at

A

Putting AI to work without losing control

Most AI pilots die between the demo and daily use. I design what surrounds the model: what it may decide, what code decides, and what each good result costs.

Models interpret, code holds the permissions. Machines check before people do, and people decide only at a few gates. I run this in my own animation studio, where agents produce children's episodes in six languages behind two human approvals.

B

Connecting physical products to data

Hardware, partners and legacy systems have to agree on what one item is and where it is. I build the platform that makes them agree.

For a global sportswear brand I owned the RFID workstream. Each item carries a unique serial that every read point, factory to store, can trace singly or in bulk. The Digital Twin keeps the ERP in sync, and a QR code on the same serial follows the item after the sale.

  • >99.9% item traceability across 100+ stores
  • −70% manual interventions once scans became events
  • $200K+ a year projected savings from the in-house serialisation MVP
C

Growth that holds up in front of finance

Growth that shows up twice: in the funnel, and in the business case that paid for it.

At a Swiss telecom operator I ran A/B tests from checkout to renewal, each tied to a KPI. For engineering investments I write NPV cases over three to five years that finance can check line by line.

  • 150% year-on-year online sales growth in 2022
  • 130 stores covered by a 3-year RFID business case

02 · How I lead

Teams, vendors and trade-offs

Teams and budgets

About 20 engineers through a year-long microservices move. A CHF 250K+ workstream with 3 vendors.

Saying no, visibly

Two business units, one platform. WSJF scoring made the trade-off visible, so no became a shared call.

Watching, not just asking

Watching stores work showed what interviews missed, so we rebuilt the data model before rollout instead of after.

Writing it down

BRDs, PRDs, story maps, and one dependency map that kept seven initiatives from blocking each other.

03 · From my studio to your automation

What running AI in production taught me

LessonResult in the studioIn your organisation
Machines check first173 issues to zero before human reviewPeople handle only what fails the checks.
Gates only where judgment mattersCut a review loop that ran to 84 versions for no gainApprove spend and customer impact, not routine steps.
Pay per usable resultA third cheaper per usable drawingCompare vendors on cost per successful task.
Test on your own dataTwo models passed 0 of 6 clips; the cheaper one passed 3 of 3Build an eval set before you sign.
