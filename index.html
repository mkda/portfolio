Connecting physical products to data

2024 to 2026 · On

Hardware, partners and legacy systems all have to agree on what a single item is and where it is. The platform that makes them agree is a product too: its APIs (the interfaces other software calls) and its events are services other teams rely on.

Problem
Stores, warehouses, vendors and the ERP each held their own view of an item.
Role
At On, the Zurich-based global sportswear brand, led the RFID technology workstream from factory shipment to 100+ stores: 5 developers, 3 vendors, a CHF 250K+ yearly budget. Contributed to the Digital Twin platform.
Result
>99.9% item traceability, up from manual barcode reads. 40M+ yearly scans turned into events, with 70% fewer manual interventions across 100+ locations. An in-house serial-number service with projected savings of $200K+ a year.

How it works in practice

Each item gets a unique serial, encoded in its RFID (radio-frequency identification) tag. Every enabled read point, from factory to store, can trace it one by one or in bulk, and the Digital Twin, a live digital record of every item, keeps the ERP (the company's finance and inventory system) in sync. A QR code paired with the same serial carries the item past the sale, into digital workflows such as its status changing from in stock to sold. Before, items were tracked by manual barcode reads. Scan events now take over the manual steps in stock counts, in stock moves to virtual locations that track financial value (damaged, sellable and non-sellable goods, visual merchandising), in inbound and outbound shipments, and in the sync with the ERP.

Scope

At On, I led the RFID technology workstream and contributed to the Digital Twin platform: an event-driven architecture on the Electronic Product Code standard, warehouse scan-point APIs, supply-chain location standards, an in-house serialisation service, and vendor capabilities brought in-house.

Where it fits in a company

Recalls narrowed to the exact batch, checks against counterfeits, stock accurate enough for buy online and pick up in store, and the Digital Product Passport the EU is introducing for textiles and other goods.

Growth that finance can check

2021 to 2026 · Sunrise, then On

Growth earns trust when it is proved twice: once in the funnel, and once in the business case that paid for it.

Problem
A telecom eShop with drop-offs hidden in its checkout funnel, and engineering budgets that had to prove their return.
Role
At Sunrise, launched a new product portfolio and the rebranded eShop. Later, at On, built the three-year business case for extending RFID to 130 stores.
Result
Online sales grew 150% year on year in 2022 (2.5 times the 2021 figure), with growth continuing in 2023.

In the funnel

At Sunrise, every test had a KPI (key performance indicator) and a tracking plan. A/B tests, which compare two versions with real customers, ran across subscriptions, prepaid, internet and devices, through checkout, ID checks, activation, upgrades and renewals. Session recordings and customer feedback explained the why behind the numbers.

In the business case

Engineering investments come with a net present value case over months or years: what future savings are worth today, the break-even year, yearly profitability, and value drivers finance can check, such as loss prevention, recalls and compliance.

Putting AI to work, with control built in

2024 to today · On and independent builds

Most AI pilots stall between the demo and daily use. The answer sits around the model: what it may decide, what code decides, how errors get caught, and what each good result costs.

Problem
AI pilots that stall between the demo and daily use.
Role
Delivered a RAG system for customs document review and worked on counterfeit QR image recognition at On. Designed and run independent AI product builds.
Result
Machine checks before every human review, two approval gates, and AI spend of $11–67 per finished episode in the studio.

The approach

Models interpret; software holds the permissions. Automated checks run before any person looks, and people decide at a few gates where judgment matters. Every model is tested on the company's own data, and when evidence is weak the system waits for more.

Proof in production

At On:

  • Customs document review. A RAG system (retrieval-augmented generation: the model answers only from approved sources) built on validated Looker reports, with quality gates that flag incorrect documents.
  • Counterfeit checks. Image recognition that reads authenticity QR codes from photos sent in by customs offices, at any angle and quality.

Independent product builds

An animation studio where agents write, draw, animate, voice and publish children's episodes in six languages, with child-safety checks on every script, video and livestream and two human approval gates. Alongside it, a decision-support prototype for market data that holds back on weak evidence and keeps orders, sizing and accounting under code control.

Where it fits in a company

Customer service replies, invoice and contract processing, store and warehouse exceptions. Agents draft, rules check, and people approve the cases that need judgment.

Teams, vendors and trade-offs

The trust equation

From The Trusted Advisor by Maister, Green and Galford. Leading across teams and vendors runs on it: numbers that check out, dates that hold, room for people to raise what worries them, and decisions made for the product rather than for credit.

Teams and budgets

About 20 engineers led through a year-long move to a microservices architecture and a complete redesign of the online experience. A workstream of 5 developers, 3 vendors and a CHF 250K+ budget.

Making trade-offs visible

When stakeholders have competing priorities, their requests are scored with WSJF (weighted shortest job first: the cost of waiting divided by the size of the job) against the engineering budget. The scores open the conversation. With the trade-off in plain view, all teams reach the right priority together.

Watching the real work

Interviews tell you how people work and where it hurts. Observation shows how they really do it, including how they handle the cases nobody planned for. That often leads to a redesigned data model, best done before rollout while the change is cheap.

Writing it down

Business requirements for sponsors, product requirements for the team, story maps to keep user journeys in view, and one dependency map across connected initiatives to keep them all moving.

Growing people

Four direct reports led for five years in a web team that shipped 300+ global campaigns a year, with a 99.4% average quality score on everything customers saw. People hired into the team later stepped into the lead role, because room was made early for them to grow into it.
