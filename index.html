Three problems, solved in production

Putting AI to work, with control built in

Most AI pilots stall between the demo and daily use. The answer sits around the model: what it may decide, what code decides, how errors get caught, and what each good result costs.

The approach

Models interpret; software holds the permissions. Automated checks run before any person looks, and people decide at a few gates where judgment matters. Every model is tested on the company's own data, and when evidence is weak the system waits for more.

Proof in production

At a global sportswear brand: a RAG system (retrieval-augmented generation, where the model answers from approved sources) built on validated Looker reports for customs document review, with quality gates that flag incorrect documents. Next to it, an image recognition tool that reads authenticity QR codes from photos sent in by customs offices, at any angle and quality, to help stop counterfeits.

Independent builds

An animation studio where agents write, draw, animate, voice and publish children's episodes in six languages, with two human approval gates. Alongside it, an experimental decision-support platform for market data that holds back on weak evidence and keeps orders, sizing and accounting under code control.

Where it fits in a company

Customer service replies, invoice and contract processing, store and warehouse exceptions. Agents draft, rules check, and people approve the cases that need judgment.

Connecting physical products to data

Hardware, partners and legacy systems all have to agree on what a single item is and where it is. The platform that makes them agree is a product too: its APIs (the interfaces other software calls) and its events are services other teams rely on.

How it works in practice

Each item gets a unique serial, encoded in its RFID (radio-frequency identification) tag. Every enabled read point, from factory to store, can trace it one by one or in bulk, and the Digital Twin, a live digital record of every item, keeps the ERP (the company's finance and inventory system) in sync. A QR code paired with the same serial carries the item past the sale, into digital workflows such as its status changing from in sale to sold.

Scope owned

The RFID and Digital Twin technology workstream for a global sportswear brand: an event-driven architecture on the Electronic Product Code standard, warehouse scan-point APIs, supply-chain location standards, an in-house serialisation service, and vendor capabilities brought in-house.

Where it fits in a company

Recalls narrowed to the exact batch, checks against counterfeits, stock accurate enough for buy online and pick up in store, and the Digital Product Passport the EU is introducing for textiles and other goods.

Growth that finance can check

Growth earns trust when it is proved twice: once in the funnel, and once in the business case that paid for it.

In the funnel

At a Swiss telecom operator, every test had a KPI (key performance indicator) and a tracking plan. A/B tests, which compare two versions with real customers, ran across subscriptions, prepaid, internet and devices, through checkout, ID checks, activation, upgrades and renewals. Session recordings and customer feedback explained the why behind the numbers.

In the business case

Engineering investments come with a net present value case over months or years: what future savings are worth today, the break-even year, yearly profitability, and value drivers finance can check, such as loss prevention, recalls and compliance.

Teams, vendors and trade-offs

The trust equation

From The Trusted Advisor by Maister, Green and Galford. Leading across teams and vendors runs on it: numbers that check out, dates that hold, room for people to raise what worries them, and decisions made for the product rather than for credit.

Teams and budgets

About 20 engineers led through a year-long move to a microservices architecture and a complete redesign of the online experience. A workstream of 5 developers, 3 vendors and a CHF 250K+ budget. A web team of 4 shipping 300+ campaigns a year.

Making trade-offs visible

When stakeholders have competing priorities, their requests are scored with WSJF (weighted shortest job first: the cost of waiting divided by the size of the job) against the engineering budget. The scores open the conversation. With the trade-off in plain view, all teams reach the right priority together.

Watching the real work

Interviews tell you how people work and where it hurts. Observation shows how they really do it, including how they handle the cases nobody planned for. That often leads to a redesigned data model, best done before rollout while the change is cheap.

Writing it down

Business requirements for sponsors, product requirements for the team, story maps to keep user journeys in view, and one dependency map across connected initiatives to keep them all moving.

What running AI in production taught me

Five lessons from running AI agents in production, and how each carries over to retail operations, telecom, customer service and internal processes. The measured results are in the AI agents tab and the playbook note.

Five lessons from the studio and how each applies in an organisation
LessonIn your organisation
Machines check first. Automated staging, continuity and lip-sync checks run before anyone reviews a cut.Check every order, ticket or document automatically, so people focus on the exceptions.
Gates only where judgment matters. Two approvals: before money is spent, and before release.Keep approvals for decisions on spend, customer impact and compliance, and let routine steps flow.
