How I lead

The trust equation

From The Trusted Advisor by Maister, Green and Galford. Leading across teams and vendors runs on it: numbers that check out, dates that hold, room for people to raise what worries them, and decisions made for the product rather than for credit.

Leading engineering teams

About 20 engineers at Sunrise, led through a year-long move to a microservices architecture and a complete redesign of the online experience. At On, a workstream of 5 developers and 3 vendors, described in the Product data case.

Making trade-offs visible

When stakeholders have competing priorities, their requests are scored with WSJF (weighted shortest job first: the cost of waiting divided by the size of the job) against the engineering budget. The scores open the conversation. With the trade-off in plain view, all teams reach the right priority together.

Writing it down

Business requirements for sponsors, product requirements for the team, story maps to keep user journeys in view, and one dependency map across connected initiatives to keep them all moving.

Growing people

Four direct reports led for five years in a web team that shipped 300+ global campaigns a year, with a 99.4% average quality score on everything customers saw. People hired into the team later stepped into the lead role, because room was made early for them to grow into it.
